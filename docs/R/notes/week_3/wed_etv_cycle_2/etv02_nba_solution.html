3 · T — Transformation 27 min

# LM3: first five seasons with complete game records
kobe_df <- nba_df |>
  filter(Season %in% paste0("season_", 1:5),
         !is.na(MP), !is.na(PTS))

nrow(kobe_df)
[1] 334
NoteExpected — LM3
  • LM3 334 complete game rows
# LM4: select what matters and create the rate
kobe_df <- kobe_df |>
  select(Season, MP, PTS) |>
  mutate(season_number = readr::parse_number(Season),
         ppm = PTS / MP)
# LM5: summarize and arrange by season_number
kobe_table_df <- kobe_df |>
  group_by(Season, season_number) |>
  summarize(games = n(),
            points = sum(PTS),
            minutes = sum(MP),
            ppm = points / minutes,
            .groups = "drop") |>
  arrange(season_number) |>
  mutate(ppm = round(ppm, 3))

ImportantTeaching note — the two plots disagree
  • The plots disagree about season 3
  • Totals decline because the season had only 50 games (lockout)
  • The rate stays near the surrounding seasons
  • That disagreement is the measurement lesson (CLO 12.2)

5 · Interpretation & final answer 10 min

TipModel answer
  • “Kobe’s scoring rate rose from about 0.496 points per minute in season 1 to about 0.697 in season 5, an increase of roughly 41%. The climb was not perfectly steady: season 3 has fewer total points because the lockout limited the schedule to 50 games, while its per-minute rate remained near 0.53. A raw total would have misled us there.”
  • Required elements (the CLO 4.5 trio for this cycle):
    • direction — rate rose
    • magnitude — ≈ 0.496 → 0.697, about +41%
    • caveat — the lockout season
